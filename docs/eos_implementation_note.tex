\section{Scope and Maintained Products}

This note documents the maintained EOS table generators and dense-EOS helper
modules in the current tree:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{scripts/generate\_saha\_table.py},
  \item \texttt{scripts/generate\_lte\_table.py},
  \item \texttt{scripts/scvh95\_eos.py},
  \item \texttt{scripts/chabrier2021\_eos.py}.
\end{itemize}
The public H/He LTE models exposed by the table generator are
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{t13},
  \item \texttt{scvh\_t13\_cp\_helm\_union},
  \item \texttt{chabrier2021\_t13\_helm\_union}.
\end{enumerate}
The separate pure-H Saha table is intentionally kept as an independent,
analytic reference EOS.  Older experimental dispatch modes have been removed
from the generator and from this note; private helper routines remain only
where a maintained union still needs them.

\section{Native Table Layout and Thermodynamic Closure}

AthenaK-native EOS tables are sampled on natural-log axes
\begin{equation}
  x \equiv \ln \rhoCGS, \qquad y \equiv \ln \TCGS,
\end{equation}
with temperature as the fastest-varying table index and
\texttt{log\_axis\_base = e}.  The runtime fields are
\begin{equation}
  \ln P,\quad \ln \eps,\quad \ln c_s^2,\quad
  \Gamma_1,\quad \gammathree,
\end{equation}
plus model-dependent composition and diagnostic fields.

For the finite-difference closures used by T13 and by dense fallback products,
the code first builds completed surfaces $P(\rho,T)$ and
$\eps_{\mathrm{phys}}(\rho,T)$ and then evaluates
\begin{align}
  \chi_\rho &= \left(\frac{\partial \ln P}{\partial \ln \rho}\right)_T,
&
  \chi_T &= \left(\frac{\partial \ln P}{\partial \ln T}\right)_\rho,
\\
  c_v &= \frac{1}{T}
        \left(\frac{\partial \eps_{\mathrm{phys}}}{\partial \ln T}\right)_\rho,
&
  \gammathree &= \frac{P\chi_T}{\rho T c_v},
\\
  \Gamma_1 &= \chi_\rho + \chi_T(\Gamma_3-1),
&
  c_s^2 &= \Gamma_1\frac{P}{\rho}.
\end{align}
The exported auxiliary heat capacity and adiabatic gradient are
\begin{equation}
  c_p = c_v\frac{\Gamma_1}{\chi_\rho},
  \qquad
  \nabla_{\mathrm{ad}} = \frac{\Gamma_3-1}{\Gamma_1}.
\end{equation}
At the two ends of a grid direction the finite differences use second-order
one-sided stencils; in the interior they use centered differences.

When photon radiation is enabled, it is added as an independent blackbody
component,
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm rad} = \frac{\arad T^4}{3}, \qquad
  \eps_{\mathrm rad} = \frac{\arad T^4}{\rho}, \qquad
  s_{\mathrm rad} = \frac{4\arad T^3}{3\rho},
\end{equation}
and the diagnostic radiation pressure fraction is
\begin{equation}
  \beta_{\mathrm rad} =
  \frac{P_{\mathrm rad}}{P_{\mathrm gas}+P_{\mathrm rad}}.
\end{equation}

\section{Pure-H Saha Reference EOS}

\texttt{generate\_saha\_table.py} solves
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm H \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{H^+}+e^-
\end{equation}
for a ground-state pure-H gas.  The partition convention cancels the electron
spin degeneracy against the neutral-H ground-state degeneracy, giving
\begin{equation}
  C_{\mathrm Saha} =
  \mh\left(\frac{2\pi\me\kb}{h^2}\right)^{3/2},
  \qquad
  y =
  \frac{C_{\mathrm Saha}T^{3/2}\exp(-T_{\mathrm ion}/T)}{\rho},
\end{equation}
where $T_{\mathrm ion}=\chi_{\mathrm H}/\kb$.  The ionization fraction
$x=n_{\mathrm{H^+}}/(n_{\mathrm H}+n_{\mathrm{H^+}})$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \frac{x^2}{1-x}=y,
  \qquad
  x = \frac{2y}{\sqrt{y^2+4y}+y},
\end{equation}
with asymptotic low- and high-$y$ branches for numerical stability.

The pressure and specific internal energy are
\begin{equation}
  P = \rho R_{\mathrm H}T(1+x),
  \qquad
  \eps = \frac{3}{2}R_{\mathrm H}T(1+x)
       + x\frac{\chi_{\mathrm H}}{\mh},
  \qquad
  R_{\mathrm H}=\frac{\kb}{\mh}.
\end{equation}
The table closure is analytic.  Defining
\begin{equation}
  g=\frac{x(1-x)}{2-x}, \qquad u_{\mathrm H}=\frac{T_{\mathrm ion}}{T},
\end{equation}
the derivatives used by the generator are
\begin{align}
  \left(\frac{\partial \eps}{\partial \rho}\right)_T
    &= -\frac{g}{\rho}
       \left(\frac{3}{2}R_{\mathrm H}T+\frac{\chi_{\mathrm H}}{\mh}\right),
\\
  \left(\frac{\partial \eps}{\partial T}\right)_\rho
    &= \frac{3}{2}R_{\mathrm H}(1+x)
       +\left(\frac{3}{2}R_{\mathrm H}T+\frac{\chi_{\mathrm H}}{\mh}\right)
        \frac{g(3/2+u_{\mathrm H})}{T},
\\
  \left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial \rho}\right)_T
    &= R_{\mathrm H}T\left[(1+x)-g\right],
\\
  \left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_\rho
    &= \rho R_{\mathrm H}\left[(1+x)+g(3/2+u_{\mathrm H})\right].
\end{align}
The adiabatic temperature derivative follows the first law,
\begin{equation}
  \left(\frac{\partial T}{\partial \rho}\right)_s =
  \frac{P/\rho^2-(\partial\eps/\partial\rho)_T}
       {(\partial\eps/\partial T)_\rho},
\end{equation}
and the stored sound speed and adiabatic exponents are
\begin{align}
  c_s^2 &=
  \left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial \rho}\right)_T
  +\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_\rho
   \left(\frac{\partial T}{\partial \rho}\right)_s,
&
  \Gamma_1 &= \frac{\rho c_s^2}{P},
&
  \gammathree &= \frac{\rho}{T}
   \left(\frac{\partial T}{\partial \rho}\right)_s.
\end{align}
This H-only EOS is not merged into the H/He union tables; its generated table is
kept byte-for-byte stable when the LTE H/He products are updated.

\section{T13 H/He Chemical-Equilibrium EOS}

\texttt{t13} implements the Tomida et al. Appendix-1 ideal
chemical-equilibrium H/He EOS with molecular hydrogen.  The species order is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{H_2},\ \mathrm H,\ \mathrm{H^+},\
  \mathrm{He},\ \mathrm{He^+},\ \mathrm{He^{2+}},\ e^-.
\end{equation}
The translational single-particle partition function is stored as
\begin{equation}
  \ln z_{\mathrm{tr}}(m,T) =
  \frac{3}{2}\ln(2\pi m\kb T)-3\ln h.
\end{equation}
For H$_2$, the rotational partition function uses a fixed 3:1 ortho/para
ratio.  With
\begin{align}
  Z_{\mathrm{even}} &=
    \sum_{J\ {\rm even}}(2J+1)
    \exp\left[-\frac{J(J+1)\theta_{\mathrm{rot}}}{2T}\right],
\\
  Z_{\mathrm{odd,shift}} &=
    \sum_{J\ {\rm odd}}3(2J+1)
    \exp\left[-\left(\frac{J(J+1)}{2}-1\right)
    \frac{\theta_{\mathrm{rot}}}{T}\right],
\end{align}
the code uses
\begin{equation}
  \ln Z_{\mathrm{rot}} =
  \frac{1}{4}\ln Z_{\mathrm{even}}
  +\frac{3}{4}\ln Z_{\mathrm{odd,shift}},
\end{equation}
with the same weights for $\partial\ln Z_{\mathrm{rot}}/\partial\ln T$.
The vibrational partition function is
\begin{equation}
  a=\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{vib}}}{2T}, \qquad
  \ln Z_{\mathrm{vib}} =
  -a-\ln\left(1-\exp[-2a]\right),
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial\ln Z_{\mathrm{vib}}}{\partial\ln T} =
  \frac{a}{\tanh a}.
\end{equation}
At large $a$ the generator uses the limiting branch
$\ln Z_{\mathrm{vib}}\simeq -a$.

The implemented species partition offsets are
\begin{align}
  \ln z_{\mathrm{H_2}} &=
    \ln z_{\mathrm{tr}}(m_{\mathrm{H_2}},T)
    +\ln Z_{\mathrm{rot}}+\ln Z_{\mathrm{vib}}+\ln4+\ln2,
\\
  \ln z_{\mathrm H} &=
    \ln z_{\mathrm{tr}}(\mh,T)+\ln2+\ln2
    -\frac{\chi_{\mathrm{dis}}}{2\kb T},
\\
  \ln z_{\mathrm{H^+}} &=
    \ln z_{\mathrm{tr}}(\mh,T)+\ln2+\ln2
    -\frac{\chi_{\mathrm{dis}}+2\chi_{\mathrm{ion,H}}}{2\kb T},
\\
  \ln z_{\mathrm{He}} &= \ln z_{\mathrm{tr}}(\mhe,T),
\\
  \ln z_{\mathrm{He^+}} &=
    \ln z_{\mathrm{tr}}(\mhe,T)-\frac{\chi_{\mathrm{ion,He1}}}{\kb T},
\\
  \ln z_{\mathrm{He^{2+}}} &=
    \ln z_{\mathrm{tr}}(\mhe,T)
    -\frac{\chi_{\mathrm{ion,He1}}+\chi_{\mathrm{ion,He2}}}{\kb T},
\\
  \ln z_e &= \ln z_{\mathrm{tr}}(\me,T)+\ln2.
\end{align}
The equilibrium constants are then
\begin{equation}
  \ln K_{\mathrm{dis}}=2\ln z_{\mathrm H}-\ln z_{\mathrm{H_2}},
  \quad
  \ln K_{\mathrm{ion,H}}=\ln z_{\mathrm{H^+}}+\ln z_e-\ln z_{\mathrm H},
\end{equation}
with analogous first and second helium ionization constants.

For each density row the generator solves the electron density by charge
neutrality.  The hydrogen atomic density is written in a stable quadratic form,
\begin{equation}
  n_{\mathrm H} =
  \frac{2n_{\mathrm{H,tot}}n_e}
  {\sqrt{(n_e+K_{\mathrm{ion,H}})^2+
  8n_{\mathrm{H,tot}}n_e^2/K_{\mathrm{dis}}}
  +n_e+K_{\mathrm{ion,H}}},
\end{equation}
followed by
\begin{equation}
  n_{\mathrm{H^+}} = n_{\mathrm H}\frac{K_{\mathrm{ion,H}}}{n_e},
  \qquad
  n_{\mathrm{H_2}} = \frac{n_{\mathrm H}^2}{K_{\mathrm{dis}}}.
\end{equation}
For helium, the normalized weights are
\begin{equation}
  w_0=1,\qquad
  w_1=\frac{K_{\mathrm{He1}}}{n_e},\qquad
  w_2=\frac{K_{\mathrm{He1}}K_{\mathrm{He2}}}{n_e^2},
\end{equation}
so that $n_{\mathrm{He}^q}=n_{\mathrm{He,tot}}w_q/(w_0+w_1+w_2)$.
The bisection root is
\begin{equation}
  n_{\mathrm{H^+}}+n_{\mathrm{He^+}}+2n_{\mathrm{He^{2+}}}-n_e=0.
\end{equation}

The ideal partition entropy per unit mass is
\begin{equation}
  s_i=\frac{\kb n_i}{\rho}
  \left[1+\frac{\partial\ln z_i}{\partial\ln T}
  -\left(\ln n_i-\ln z_i\right)\right].
\end{equation}
Before pair replacement the partition internal-energy density is
\begin{equation}
  u_{\mathrm{part}} =
  \kb T\sum_i n_i\frac{\partial\ln z_i}{\partial\ln T}.
\end{equation}
The diagnostic energy components are assembled as
\begin{align}
  u_{\mathrm{trans}} &= \frac{3}{2}(n_{\mathrm{tot}}-n_e)\kb T
                       + u_{e^\pm,\mathrm{kin}},
\\
  u_{\mathrm{rot}} &= n_{\mathrm{H_2}}\kb T
    \frac{\partial\ln Z_{\mathrm{rot}}}{\partial\ln T},
&
  u_{\mathrm{vib}} &= n_{\mathrm{H_2}}\kb T
    \frac{\partial\ln Z_{\mathrm{vib}}}{\partial\ln T},
\\
  u_{\mathrm{zpe}} &= n_{\mathrm{H_2}}E_{\mathrm{zpe}},
&
  u_{\mathrm{diss}} &= \frac{1}{2}\chi_{\mathrm{dis}}
    (n_{\mathrm H}+n_{\mathrm{H^+}}),
\\
  u_{\mathrm{ion,H}} &= \chi_{\mathrm{ion,H}}n_{\mathrm{H^+}},
&
  u_{\mathrm{ion,He1}} &=
    \chi_{\mathrm{ion,He1}}(n_{\mathrm{He^+}}+n_{\mathrm{He^{2+}}}),
\\
  u_{\mathrm{ion,He2}} &= \chi_{\mathrm{ion,He2}}n_{\mathrm{He^{2+}}}.
\end{align}
The gas pressure is
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{gas}}=(n_{\mathrm{tot}}-n_e)\kb T+P_{e^\pm}.
\end{equation}
For the standalone \texttt{t13} runtime table, the optional zero-point-energy
convention is
\begin{equation}
  \eps_{\mathrm{runtime}} =
  \eps_{\mathrm{physical}} -
  X\frac{E_{\mathrm{zpe}}}{2\mh},
\end{equation}
while \texttt{eps\_physical} remains exported.  In the union tables this
subtraction is disabled so all branches use the same physical energy zero.

\section{Relativistic Electron-Positron Pair Gas}

The H/He LTE generator includes an ideal relativistic $e^-/e^+$ gas following
the HELM/MESA convention.  It enforces
\begin{equation}
  n_{e^-}-n_{e^+}=n_{\mathrm{charge}},
\end{equation}
where $n_{\mathrm{charge}}$ is fixed by the baryonic ion charge.  The electron
degeneracy parameter $\eta$ excludes rest mass, and the positron relation uses
$-\eta-2/\theta$ with
\begin{equation}
  \theta=\frac{\kb T}{\me\clight^2}, \qquad
  \lambda_e=\frac{\hbar}{\me\clight}.
\end{equation}
Using generalized Laguerre nodes $y_j$ and weights $w_j$ with index
$\alpha=1/2$, the code evaluates
\begin{equation}
  \gamma_j = 1+\theta y_j,\qquad
  r_j=\sqrt{2+\theta y_j},
\end{equation}
and stable Fermi factors equivalent to
\begin{equation}
  f^-_j = \frac{1}{\exp(y_j-\eta)+1},
  \qquad
  f^+_j = \frac{1}{\exp(y_j+\eta+2/\theta)+1}.
\end{equation}
The densities are
\begin{equation}
  n_\mp =
  \frac{\theta^{3/2}}{\pi^2\lambda_e^3}
  \sum_j w_j r_j\gamma_j f^\mp_j .
\end{equation}
The pressure and kinetic-energy densities are
\begin{align}
  P_\mp &=
  \frac{\me\clight^2\theta^{5/2}}{3\pi^2\lambda_e^3}
  \sum_j w_j y_j r_j^3 f^\mp_j,
\\
  u_{\mathrm{kin},\mp} &=
  \frac{\me\clight^2\theta^{5/2}}{\pi^2\lambda_e^3}
  \sum_j w_j y_j r_j\gamma_j f^\mp_j.
\end{align}
The created-pair rest-mass convention used in the table is
\begin{equation}
  u_{\mathrm{rest,pair}} = 2\me\clight^2 n_{e^+}.
\end{equation}
This includes the rest mass of newly created pairs only, not the rest mass of
the charge-balancing electrons already tied to the baryonic composition.  The
lepton entropy density is evaluated from
\begin{equation}
  s_{e^\pm} =
  \frac{u_{e^\pm}+P_{e^\pm}-\eta\kb T n_{\mathrm{charge}}}{T},
\end{equation}
and clipped non-negative.

The pair diagnostics written to H/He tables are
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{pair,diag}}=2P_{e^+}, \qquad
  f_{\mathrm{pair}}=\frac{P_{\mathrm{pair,diag}}}{P_{\mathrm{tot}}},
\end{equation}
stored as \texttt{ppair} and \texttt{pair\_pressure\_fraction}, plus pair
energy, $n_{e^-}$, $n_{e^+}$, and $\eta$ diagnostics.  In \texttt{t13}, the
pair gas replaces the classical ideal electron contribution only for
$\log_{10}(T/{\rm K})\ge 7$.  In the fully ionized HELM and Coulomb fallback
branches, pairs are part of the thermodynamics throughout the branch.  In
native SCvH and Chabrier dense cells, the imported dense-fluid thermodynamics
are not rewritten by pair production; the same HELM-style pair calculation is
overlaid only as a diagnostic map.

\section{Fully Ionized HELM Fallback}

The full local HELM dense fallback assumes fully ionized H/He,
\begin{equation}
  n_{\mathrm H}=\frac{X\rho}{\mh},\qquad
  n_{\mathrm{He}}=\frac{Y\rho}{\mhe},\qquad
  n_{\mathrm{charge}}=n_{\mathrm H}+2n_{\mathrm{He}},
\end{equation}
and $n_{\mathrm{ions}}=n_{\mathrm H}+n_{\mathrm{He}}$.  It includes ideal
ions, the arbitrary-relativity/arbitrary-degeneracy electron--positron gas,
photons, and the Potekhin--Chabrier liquid ion--ion Coulomb free energy below.
The gas pressure is
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{gas}}=n_{\mathrm{ions}}\kb T+P_{e^\pm}+P_C.
\end{equation}
The specific gas energy is
\begin{equation}
  \eps_{\mathrm{gas}} =
  \frac{(3/2)n_{\mathrm{ions}}\kb T+u_{e^\pm}+u_C}{\rho}
  + \eps_{\mathrm{offset}},
\end{equation}
with an energy-zero offset matched to the fully ionized T13 limit,
\begin{equation}
  \eps_{\mathrm{offset}} =
  X\frac{\chi_{\mathrm{dis}}/2+\chi_{\mathrm{ion,H}}}{\mh}
  +Y\frac{\chi_{\mathrm{ion,He1}}+\chi_{\mathrm{ion,He2}}}{\mhe}.
\end{equation}
Ion entropies use the ideal translational form
\begin{equation}
  s_i=\frac{\kb n_i}{\rho}
  \left[\frac{5}{2}-\left(\ln n_i-\ln z_{\mathrm{tr},i}\right)\right],
\end{equation}
and pair entropy is added separately.  The branch is accepted only where the
simple LTE ionization proxy gives
\begin{equation}
  x_{\mathrm{H^+}}\ge 0.99,\qquad x_{\mathrm{He^{2+}}}\ge 0.90.
\end{equation}

The Coulomb term uses a Potekhin--Chabrier liquid fit.
With electron-sphere radius
\begin{equation}
  a_e=\left(\frac{3}{4\pi n_e}\right)^{1/3},
\end{equation}
the ion coupling parameters are
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma_{\mathrm H}=\frac{e^2}{a_e\kb T},\qquad
  \Gamma_{\mathrm{He}}=\frac{2^{5/3}e^2}{a_e\kb T}.
\end{equation}
The reduced liquid free-energy fit implemented in the generator is
\begin{align}
  f_{ii}(\Gamma) &=
  a_1\left[
  \sqrt{\Gamma(a_2+\Gamma)}
  -a_2\ln\left(\sqrt{\Gamma/a_2}+\sqrt{1+\Gamma/a_2}\right)
  \right]
\\
  &\quad
  +2a_3\left(\sqrt{\Gamma}-\tan^{-1}\sqrt{\Gamma}\right)
  +b_1\left[\Gamma-b_2\ln(1+\Gamma/b_2)\right]
  +\frac{b_3}{2}\ln(1+\Gamma^2/b_4),
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}
  a_1=-0.907347,\quad a_2=0.62849,\quad
  a_3=-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}-\frac{a_1}{\sqrt{a_2}},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  b_1=0.00456,\quad b_2=211.6,\quad
  b_3=-10^{-4},\quad b_4=0.00462.
\end{equation}
The code computes
$u_{ii}=\partial f_{ii}/\partial\ln\Gamma$ analytically, avoiding the
weak-coupling cancellation of a centered finite difference.
The Coulomb thermodynamic additions are
\begin{align}
  P_C &= \frac{\kb T}{3}\sum_i n_i u_{ii,i},
&
  \eps_C &= \frac{\kb T}{\rho}\sum_i n_i u_{ii,i},
&
  s_C &= \frac{\kb}{\rho}\sum_i n_i(u_{ii,i}-f_{ii,i}).
\end{align}
The gap-filling HELM corner is gated by full ionization.  In the Chabrier
union, HELM is instead preferred for $\rho\ge0.1\,\mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}}$ and
$T\ge10^8\,\mathrm K$, where the high-temperature switch supersedes the
low-density Saha ionization proxy.  Both paths require
$\Gamma_{\mathrm{He}}\le175$,
and
\begin{equation}
  T \ge 3\max(T_{p,\mathrm H},T_{p,\mathrm{He}}),
  \qquad
  T_{p,i}=\frac{\hbar}{\kb}
  \left(\frac{4\pi n_i Z_i^2e^2}{m_i}\right)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}

\section{SCvH 1995 Union}

\texttt{scvh\_t13\_cp\_helm\_union} combines T13, SCvH, the Coulomb corner,
and the HELM fallback.  The SCvH helper reads the original hydrogen and helium
tables in native $(\log_{10}T,\log_{10}P)$ coordinates, including the hydrogen
plasma phase-transition branches.  For a target isotherm it builds a composite
pressure grid, inserts the low- and high-density PPT branches where applicable,
mixes H and He, and then resamples to the AthenaK $\log\rho$ grid with PCHIP.

The H/He volume mixing is the additive-volume law
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{\rho}=\frac{X}{\rho_{\mathrm H}}+\frac{Y}{\rho_{\mathrm{He}}},
\end{equation}
and the internal energy is
\begin{equation}
  \eps_{\mathrm{gas}}=X\eps_{\mathrm H}+Y\eps_{\mathrm{He}}.
\end{equation}
The entropy is
\begin{equation}
  s_{\mathrm{gas}}=Xs_{\mathrm H}+Ys_{\mathrm{He}}+s_{\mathrm{mix}},
\end{equation}
where $s_{\mathrm{mix}}$ is the SCvH composition-dependent ideal mixing
correction evaluated from the tabulated H$_2$/H/H$^+$ and
He/He$^+$/He$^{2+}$ fractions and their local derivatives in $T$ and $P$.
The code keeps the full SCvH algebra for this correction rather than replacing
it with a composition-independent ideal-gas approximation.

For the gas-only SCvH mixture, the helper computes
\begin{equation}
  \alpha =
  \rho\left[
  \frac{X}{\rho_{\mathrm H}}
  \left(\frac{\partial\ln\rho_{\mathrm H}}{\partial\ln T}\right)_P
  +\frac{Y}{\rho_{\mathrm{He}}}
  \left(\frac{\partial\ln\rho_{\mathrm{He}}}{\partial\ln T}\right)_P
  \right],
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \beta =
  \rho\left[
  \frac{X}{\rho_{\mathrm H}}
  \left(\frac{\partial\ln\rho_{\mathrm H}}{\partial\ln P}\right)_T
  +\frac{Y}{\rho_{\mathrm{He}}}
  \left(\frac{\partial\ln\rho_{\mathrm{He}}}{\partial\ln P}\right)_T
  \right],
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}
  \chi_{\rho,\mathrm{gas}}=\frac{1}{\beta},\qquad
  \chi_{T,\mathrm{gas}}=-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}.
\end{equation}
The gas adiabatic gradient follows the mixed entropy derivatives,
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_{\mathrm{ad,gas}} =
  -\frac{(\partial\ln s/\partial\ln P)_T}
        {(\partial\ln s/\partial\ln T)_P},
\end{equation}
and the gas exponents are
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma_{1,\mathrm{gas}}=
  \frac{\chi_{\rho,\mathrm{gas}}}
       {1-\chi_{T,\mathrm{gas}}\nabla_{\mathrm{ad,gas}}},
  \qquad
  (\Gamma_3-1)_{\mathrm{gas}}=
  \frac{\chi_{\rho,\mathrm{gas}}\nabla_{\mathrm{ad,gas}}}
       {1-\chi_{T,\mathrm{gas}}\nabla_{\mathrm{ad,gas}}}.
\end{equation}
Radiation is then added through the same additive formulas as above.  The
helper rejects cells whose imported thermodynamic derivatives disagree too
strongly with finite differences on the resampled table.

The union ownership rule is
\begin{enumerate}
  \item use valid SCvH cells wherever available,
  \item otherwise use the fully ionized Coulomb corner where its gates pass,
  \item otherwise use the HELM fully ionized pair fallback in hot dense cells,
  \item otherwise use T13 on the low-density side,
  \item fill remaining rectangular holes from T13 or HELM and enforce a
        strictly increasing $\ln\eps$ envelope along each temperature row.
\end{enumerate}
The source-map fields are \texttt{source\_t13}, \texttt{source\_scvh},
\texttt{source\_corner\_cp}, \texttt{source\_corner\_helm},
\texttt{source\_corner}, \texttt{source\_filler}, and
\texttt{eps\_envelope\_correction}.

\section{Chabrier 2021 Dense-Fluid Union}

\texttt{chabrier2021\_t13\_helm\_union} is the current default dense H/He EOS
product.  It covers
\begin{equation}
  -20 \le \log_{10}\rho_{\mathrm{cgs}} \le 3,\qquad
  0 \le \log_{10}(T/{\rm K}) \le 10
\end{equation}
in the hardcoded 640 by 640 job.  The dense-fluid input tables are available
at helium mass fractions $Y=0.275,0.292,0.297$; the helper linearly
interpolates or, for the local default $Y=0.30$, mildly extrapolates in $Y$.

The native Chabrier rows are in $(\log_{10}T,\log_{10}P_{\mathrm{GPa}})$.
They contain $\log_{10}\rho$, $\log_{10}u$, $\log_{10}s$,
the two density derivatives at constant $P$ and $T$, and
$\nabla_{\mathrm{ad}}$.  Unit conversion is
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{gas}}=10^{\log_{10}P_{\mathrm{GPa}}+10},
  \qquad
  \eps_{\mathrm{gas}}=10^{\log_{10}u_{\mathrm{MJ\,kg^{-1}}}}10^{10},
  \qquad
  s_{\mathrm{gas}}=10^{\log_{10}s_{\mathrm{MJ\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}}}10^{10}.
\end{equation}
For each target isotherm, valid native cells are filtered, forced monotonic in
$\log\rho$, and linearly interpolated to the target $\log\rho$ grid.

The gas derivatives are
\begin{equation}
  \chi_{\rho,\mathrm{gas}} =
  \left[
  \left(\frac{\partial\ln\rho}{\partial\ln P}\right)_T
  \right]^{-1},
  \qquad
  \chi_{T,\mathrm{gas}} =
  -\frac{(\partial\ln\rho/\partial\ln T)_P}
        {(\partial\ln\rho/\partial\ln P)_T},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{\mathrm{gas}} =
  -\left(\frac{\partial\ln\rho}{\partial\ln T}\right)_P.
\end{equation}
The heat capacities are reconstructed as
\begin{equation}
  c_{p,\mathrm{gas}} =
  \frac{P_{\mathrm{gas}}\delta_{\mathrm{gas}}}
       {\rho T\nabla_{\mathrm{ad,gas}}},
  \qquad
  c_{v,\mathrm{gas}} =
  c_{p,\mathrm{gas}} -
  \frac{P_{\mathrm{gas}}\chi_{T,\mathrm{gas}}^2}
       {\rho T\chi_{\rho,\mathrm{gas}}}.
\end{equation}
After adding radiation, the closure is
\begin{align}
  \chi_\rho &=
  \frac{P_{\mathrm{gas}}\chi_{\rho,\mathrm{gas}}}{P_{\mathrm{tot}}},
&
  \chi_T &=
  \frac{P_{\mathrm{gas}}\chi_{T,\mathrm{gas}}+4P_{\mathrm{rad}}}
       {P_{\mathrm{tot}}},
\\
  c_v &= c_{v,\mathrm{gas}}+\frac{4\arad T^3}{\rho},
&
  \gammathree &= \frac{P_{\mathrm{tot}}\chi_T}{\rho T c_v},
\\
  \Gamma_1 &= \chi_\rho+\chi_T(\Gamma_3-1),
&
  c_s^2 &= \Gamma_1\frac{P_{\mathrm{tot}}}{\rho}.
\end{align}

The union rule is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item build T13 everywhere as the low-density chemical EOS,
  \item import and resample the Chabrier dense-fluid EOS,
  \item fill tiny dense Chabrier holes row-by-row with monotone PCHIP or
        nearest valid edge values,
  \item overlay HELM pair diagnostics on valid Chabrier cells without changing
        their pressure, energy, or adiabatic indices,
  \item blend T13 and Chabrier over
        $10^{-3}\le\rho_{\mathrm{cgs}}<10^{-2}$ with smootherstep weights,
  \item additionally blend the first few dense Chabrier onset cells in
        temperature to reduce a visible $\Gamma_1$ boundary,
  \item use pure Chabrier cells at higher density wherever valid,
  \item use the HELM fully ionized gap fallback above
        $\log_{10}(T/{\rm K})\ge 7$ where Chabrier is not valid,
  \item prefer full HELM for $\rho\ge0.1\,\mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}}$ above
        $\log_{10}(T/{\rm K})\ge 8$, even if a Chabrier row remains available.
\end{enumerate}
The smootherstep weight is
\begin{equation}
  S(q)=6q^5-15q^4+10q^3,\qquad 0<q<1,
\end{equation}
with $S=0$ below the interval and $S=1$ above it.  Linear physical fields are
blended as $(1-S)A+SB$; logarithmic runtime fields are then rebuilt from the
blended pressure, energy, and sound speed.  On pure Chabrier dense cells,
species fractions and $\mu$ are diagnostic proxies from the simple atomic H/He
Saha solver and do not feed back into the Chabrier thermodynamics.

\section{Validation and Generated Artifacts}

Before writing, each table must have finite positive runtime fields, positive
$c_s^2$, positive $\Gamma_1$ and $\Gamma_3-1$, positive $\mu$, bounded species
fractions, bounded $\beta_{\mathrm rad}$, and non-negative pair-pressure
diagnostics.  Union tables additionally validate binary non-overlapping source
masks, finite pair diagnostics, and strictly increasing $\ln\eps$ along every
temperature row after any rectangular filling.

The maintained generated tables live under \texttt{eos\_tables/}.  The
Chabrier 640 by 640 diagnostic table can exceed GitHub's normal 100 MB object
limit and should be handled through Git LFS or an external artifact store if it
needs to be versioned.

\section{Source Literature Encoded by the Implementation}

The H/He low-density chemistry follows the Tomida et al. Appendix-1
partition-function EOS as used by the local Andalman-style workflow.  Dense
low-temperature H/He fluid physics comes either from Saumon, Chabrier, and Van
Horn 1995 or from the Chabrier et al. 2021 H/He tables.  The fully ionized
fallback pair thermodynamics follow the ideal relativistic HELM/MESA
electron-positron gas convention associated with Timmes and Swesty/Timmes and
Arnett.  The optional Coulomb corner uses a Potekhin-Chabrier-style liquid
free-energy fit for fully ionized ions.  The table metadata records the exact
source URLs and scalar constants used for a generated table.
